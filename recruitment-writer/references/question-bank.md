# Behavioural question bank by competency

Starting points, not a script to use verbatim — adapt to the role. Each follows the same pattern:
past situation, not hypothetical or opinion.

## Prioritisation / time management

- "Tell me about a time you had two urgent deadlines at once. How did you decide what came first?"
- "Describe a project where the scope changed midway. What did you do?"
- Weak alternative to avoid: "Are you good at managing your time?" (invites a rehearsed yes)

## Collaboration / conflict

- "Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague or manager about how to approach something. What
  happened?"
- "Describe a time you had to get buy-in from someone who didn't report to you."
- Weak alternative to avoid: "Do you work well with others?"

## Ownership / accountability

- "Tell me about a mistake you made that had real consequences. What did you do after you found
  out?"
- "Describe a time something went wrong that wasn't directly your fault, but you were closest to it."
- Follow-up that matters: ask what changed afterward, not just what happened — an answer with no
  lasting change is a weaker signal than one with a concrete adjustment.

## Communication under pressure

- "Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news to a stakeholder or client."
- "Describe explaining something technical to someone without that background. How did you check
  they understood?"

## Adaptability

- "Tell me about a time the plan changed significantly partway through a project."
- "Describe a tool, process, or role change you initially resisted. What changed your mind, if
  anything?"

## Leadership (for roles with any supervisory or influence component)

- "Tell me about a time you had to motivate someone who was disengaged."
- "Describe a decision you made that was unpopular with your team. How did you handle the reaction?"

## Technical/role-specific competencies

Behavioural questions work less well here than practical assessment — a take-home exercise, a live
problem-solving session, or a portfolio review usually gives a better signal than "tell me about a
time you used [specific tool]." Reserve behavioural questions for how the person approaches problems
(debugging under pressure, learning a new system), not for verifying specific technical knowledge.

## Follow-up probes that improve any answer

- "What specifically did you say/do at that point?" — moves from a vague narrative to observable
  behaviour.
- "What would you do differently now?" — tests self-awareness without requiring a mistake to be
  named upfront.
- "How did the other person/team react?" — checks whether the candidate is aware of impact beyond
  their own actions.
