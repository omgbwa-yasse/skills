# Process approach, PDCA, and risk-based thinking

The three concepts that structure ISO 9001:2015. An analysis that treats the clauses as an
independent list misses the system.

---

## The seven quality management principles

Drawn from ISO 9000:2015, they underpin the standard without being auditable themselves:

customer focus · leadership · engagement of people · process approach · improvement · evidence-based
decision making · relationship management.

Useful for explaining *why* a requirement exists. Never usable as the basis for a finding.

---

## The process approach

Understanding and managing interacting processes as a **system**. The point is controlling
interactions and interdependencies, not describing each process in isolation — most failures happen
at the interfaces.

**Elements of a process.** Inputs → activities → outputs, with the monitoring and measurement points
needed for control. These points are specific to each process and vary with the associated risks
(4.4.1 c and f): this is where risk-based thinking concretely enters process design.

**What 4.4.1 requires for each process** — the grid to apply to any process sheet:

| Question | Clause |
|---|---|
| What required inputs, what expected outputs? | 4.4.1 a |
| What is its place in the sequence, what interactions with the others? | 4.4.1 b |
| What criteria, methods, monitoring, measurements, and performance indicators? | 4.4.1 c |
| What resources, and are they available? | 4.4.1 d |
| Who is responsible, with what authority? | 4.4.1 e |
| What risks and opportunities are addressed? | 4.4.1 f |
| How is the process evaluated and changed if results fall short? | 4.4.1 g |
| How is it improved? | 4.4.1 h |

A process sheet that doesn't answer all eight questions is incomplete against 4.4.1.

### Mapping tools

**Turtle diagram** — a process at the centre, inputs on the left, outputs on the right, and four
questions around it: with what (equipment, materials), with whom (competence), how (methods,
procedures), how much (indicators). It naturally covers the requirements of 4.4.1; it is the most
effective tool for a process audit.

**SIPOC** — Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers. Useful for scoping a process at the
outset and spotting upstream-downstream interfaces.

**Overall process map** — three families: management processes (steering, management review,
improvement), realisation processes (those that produce value for the customer), support processes
(HR, purchasing, IT, infrastructure). The arrows between processes matter as much as the boxes: they
represent the interactions required by 4.4.1 b.

**Frequent pitfall.** Mapping the org chart instead of the processes. A process crosses departments;
if it stops at a department's boundary, it's a function, not a process.

## The PDCA cycle

Applicable to every process and to the QMS as a whole.

- **Plan** — establish the system's objectives, its processes, and the resources needed to deliver
  results that conform to customer requirements and the organisation's policies.
- **Do** — implement what was planned.
- **Check** — monitor and, where applicable, measure processes and the resulting products and
  services against policies, objectives, and requirements, and report the results.
- **Act** — take actions to improve performance, as necessary.

**Mapping to the clauses:**

| Phase | Clauses |
|---|---|
| Framing | 4 Context of the organization, 5 Leadership |
| Plan | 6 Planning |
| Do | 7 Support, 8 Operation |
| Check | 9 Performance evaluation |
| Act | 10 Improvement |

Quick maturity diagnostic for a QMS: where does the cycle break down? Most often between Check and
Act — plenty of data, no decisions. Sometimes between Plan and Do — stated objectives with no means
to achieve them.

## Risk-based thinking

It enables an organisation to determine the factors that could cause its processes and QMS to
deviate from planned results, to put in place preventive control to limit negative effects, and to
make maximum use of opportunities.

It replaces the preventive action of earlier editions, by building it into planning rather than
treating it as a separate procedure.

**Risk is the effect of uncertainty, and that effect can be positive or negative.** A positive
deviation arising from a risk can provide an opportunity — but not all positive effects of risk
result in opportunities. Opportunities can arise from a favourable situation: attracting customers,
developing new products and services, reducing waste, improving productivity, opening new markets,
building partnerships, adopting new technology.

**What the standard does not impose:** no method, no scoring, no register, no requirement to comply
with ISO 31000. See `clause-requirements.md`, clause 6.1.

**What it does require:** that risks and opportunities be determined from the context (4.1) and
interested parties (4.2); that actions be planned; that they be integrated into the QMS processes;
that their effectiveness be evaluated; and that they be **proportionate to the potential impact on
the conformity of products and services**.

## The through-line

The logical chain running through the whole standard, and the best coherence test for a QMS:

```
4.1 issues + 4.2 interested parties
        ↓
4.3 scope
        ↓
4.4 processes, with criteria and indicators
        ↓
6.1 risks and opportunities → actions integrated into processes
        ↓
6.2 measurable quality objectives, broken down to processes
        ↓
7 resources, competence, documented information
        ↓
8 operation under controlled conditions
        ↓
9.1 monitoring and measurement → 9.2 internal audit → 9.3 management review
        ↓
10 nonconformities, corrective actions, continual improvement
        ↓
        back to 4.1: have the issues changed?
```

**The coherence test.** Take an issue identified in 4.1 and follow it all the way through: did it
produce a risk or an opportunity in 6.1? An action? Is that action integrated into a process? Is its
effectiveness evaluated in 9.1.3 e) and presented at management review (9.3.2 e)?

Then take a nonconformity in 10.2 and trace it back: did it update the risks and opportunities
(10.2.1 e)? Did it change the QMS where necessary (10.2.1 f)?

Where the chain breaks is where the real problem lies — and that piece of information is worth more
than a clause-by-clause list of findings.
