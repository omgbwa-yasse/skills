# Documented information required by ISO 9001:2015

The full inventory of what the standard explicitly requires. Everything else falls under what the
organisation itself determines is necessary for the effectiveness of its QMS (7.5.1 b) — and that
latitude is real, not a loophole.

## The founding distinction

| Wording | Nature | Practical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Maintain** documented information | A living document | It exists, is kept up to date, and stands as the current version |
| **Retain** documented information | Fixed evidence | It attests to what happened at a given moment; it is not modified |

ISO 9001:2015's vocabulary replaced "document" and "record," but the functional distinction remains.
A quality policy is maintained; an audit result is retained.

| Clause | Subject |

Only four items. Any additional requirement for a permanent document raised in an analysis must be
justified by 7.5.1 b) — i.e., by what the organisation itself determines is necessary — not presented
as a requirement of the standard.

## What the organisation adds on its own

Under 7.5.1 b), most organisations also keep — without being required to — a process map, process
sheets or procedures, work instructions, forms, a risk-and-opportunity register, an
interested-parties matrix, a competence matrix, a communication plan.

None of these are required as such. However, once the organisation has built them into its QMS, they
become internal requirements, and the internal audit (9.2.1 a.1) must check conformity against these
own requirements as much as against the standard's. An organisation that over-documents thereby
creates its own non-conformities — the argument to use when a user wants to "document everything just
to be safe."

## Control to check (7.5.3)

For any documented information in the QMS:

- Available and suitable for use, where and when it is needed
- Protected: confidentiality, improper use, loss of integrity
- Distribution, access, retrieval, and use defined
- Storage and protection, including preservation of legibility over time
- Control of changes, including version control
- Retention period and disposal arrangements defined
- Externally originated information (standards, customer requirements, regulatory texts) identified
  and controlled
- Evidence of conformity protected from unintended alteration

Points most often found lacking: control of externally originated documents, retention periods,
long-term legibility of digital media, and protection of records against modification.
