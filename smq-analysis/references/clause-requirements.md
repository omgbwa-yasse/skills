# Requirements, clause by clause (ISO 9001:2015)

An operational restatement of clauses 4 through 10. For each clause: what is required, the evidence
to look for, frequent gaps.

> purposes, not a transcription. For certification work, refer to the official copy of the standard.

## What the standard does NOT require

To check before making any finding. These requirements existed in ISO 9001:2008 and disappeared in
2015. Demanding them is the most common error, and it immediately discredits an analysis.

- **No quality manual.** The organisation may keep one; nothing requires it to.
- **No six mandatory documented procedures.** The organisation determines for itself what documented
  information is needed for its QMS to be effective, in addition to what the standard names explicitly.
- **No management representative.** The corresponding responsibilities must be assigned (5.3), but
  not concentrated in a single designated person.
- **No separate "preventive action."** It is replaced by the risk-based approach of clause 6.1,
  built into planning.
- **No formal risk register, nor any obligation to apply ISO 31000.** See 6.1.
- **No mandated terminology.** The organisation is not required to use the standard's own terms in
  its documents; "supplier" instead of "external provider" is not a gap.
- **No document structure copying the clauses.** The QMS does not have to follow the standard's
  layout.
- **No written procedure required** where the standard only asks the organisation to "determine,"
  "ensure," or "take into account."

Also distinguish verb forms: **"shall"** = an auditable requirement; **"should"** = a recommendation,
not auditable; **"may"** = a possibility or permission. NOTEs clarify but add no requirement — a
finding based on a NOTE is not defensible.

## Clause 4 — Context of the organization

### 4.1 Understanding the organization and its context

**Required.** Determine external and internal issues relevant to the organisation's purpose and
strategic direction, and that affect its ability to achieve the intended results of the QMS. Monitor
and review information about these issues.

Issues can be positive or negative. External context covers the legal, technological, competitive,
market, cultural, social, and economic environment, at international, national, regional, and local
scale. Internal context covers values, culture, knowledge, and performance.

**Evidence to look for.** A SWOT or PESTEL analysis, strategic meeting minutes, a business plan,
management-review input data on how the context has evolved. No format is imposed and no documented
information is required here — but without a trace, the organisation will struggle to demonstrate
monitoring and review.

**Frequent gaps.** An analysis done once at QMS launch and never revisited. Issues stated so generally
("competition," "the economy") that no consequence is drawn from them. No link to 6.1: identified
issues that generate no risk or opportunity.

### 4.2 Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties

**Required.** Determine the interested parties relevant to the QMS, and their relevant requirements —
"relevant" meaning they have a real or potential effect on the ability to consistently provide
products and services that meet customer and applicable statutory and regulatory requirements. Monitor
and review this information.

**Evidence to look for.** A list or matrix of interested parties with their requirements; regulatory
watch; tracking of contractual requirements.

**Frequent gaps.** An exhaustive list of every imaginable party, with no relevance filter — the
opposite of what's asked. Statutory and regulatory requirements neither identified nor kept up to
date. No review at all.

### 4.3 Determining the scope of the QMS

**Required.** Determine the boundaries and applicability of the QMS considering the issues (4.1), the
requirements of relevant interested parties (4.2), and the products and services. Apply all applicable
requirements of the standard. Keep the scope up to date **as documented information**, stating the
types of products and services covered and justifying any requirement deemed not applicable.

**Hard condition.** Conformity may only be claimed if the excluded requirements do not affect the
organisation's ability or responsibility to ensure the conformity of its products and services and to
enhance customer satisfaction.

**Evidence to look for.** The scope document, with justification for any non-applicability.

**Frequent gaps.** Clause 8.3 (design and development) excluded by an organisation that genuinely
designs, often because it doesn't call that activity "design." Justification missing or reduced to
"not applicable." Scope inconsistent with actual activities or with the certificate sought. Sites or
activities excluded with no basis.

### 4.4 The QMS and its processes

**Required.** Establish, implement, maintain, and continually improve the QMS, including the
processes needed and their interactions. For these processes: determine the required inputs and
expected outputs; determine their sequence and interaction; determine and apply the criteria,
methods, monitoring, measurements, and related performance indicators needed to ensure their effective
operation and control; determine and provide the resources; assign responsibilities and authorities;
address the risks and opportunities from 6.1; evaluate the processes and implement any changes needed;
improve the processes and the system.

Maintain documented information needed to support the operation of processes, and retain documented
information giving confidence that the processes are carried out as planned — **to the extent
necessary**, a wording that leaves room for the organisation's own judgement.

**Evidence to look for.** Process map, process sheets, indicators and their results, a
responsibility matrix.

**Frequent gaps.** A decorative process map, with no criteria or indicators. Indicators defined but
not tracked, or tracked but never used. Interactions between processes not described. A gap between
the processes as described and how things actually run — this is the most revealing thing in an
audit.

## Clause 5 — Leadership

### 5.1.1 Leadership and commitment (general)

**Required.** Top management demonstrates leadership and commitment by: taking accountability for the
effectiveness of the QMS; ensuring the quality policy and objectives are established and compatible
with the context and strategic direction; ensuring the integration of the QMS requirements **into the
organisation's business processes**; promoting the process approach and risk-based thinking; ensuring
resources are available; communicating the importance of effective quality management and of
conforming to the QMS requirements; ensuring the QMS achieves its intended results; engaging,
directing, and supporting people; promoting improvement; supporting other relevant management roles
in their responsibilities.

**Evidence to look for.** Interviews with top management — the primary and often only evidence.
Management-review minutes, resource arbitrations, internal communications, actual participation in
quality governance.

**Frequent gaps.** The QMS delegated to the quality manager and disconnected from how the business is
actually run, which contradicts the requirement to integrate it into business processes. Top
management unable to state the quality policy or the QMS's intended results. Resources refused with
no traceable arbitration.

### 5.1.2 Customer focus

**Required.** Top management ensures that customer and applicable statutory and regulatory
requirements are determined, understood, and consistently met; that the risks and opportunities that
can affect conformity of products and services and the ability to enhance customer satisfaction are
determined and addressed; that the focus on enhancing customer satisfaction is maintained.

### 5.2 Quality policy

**Required.** Establish, implement, and maintain a quality policy appropriate to the purpose and
context, supporting strategic direction, providing a framework for setting quality objectives, and
including a commitment to satisfy applicable requirements and a commitment to continual improvement
of the QMS.

The policy must be **maintained as documented information**, communicated, understood, and applied
within the organisation, and made available to relevant interested parties as appropriate.

**Frequent gaps.** A generic policy, interchangeable with any other organisation's, unconnected to
context. Posted but not understood — an interview with two or three people reveals this immediately.
Provides no usable framework for objectives. The two required commitments not stated explicitly.

### 5.3 Organizational roles, responsibilities and authorities

**Required.** Top management ensures responsibilities and authorities are assigned, communicated, and
understood. It assigns responsibility and authority for: ensuring the QMS conforms to the standard's
requirements; ensuring processes deliver their intended outputs; reporting on QMS performance and
improvement opportunities to top management; promoting customer focus throughout the organisation;
maintaining the integrity of the QMS when changes are planned and implemented.

**Frequent gap.** Assigned on paper, not understood in practice. Reminder: no management
representative is required, but these five responsibilities must genuinely be assigned to someone.

## Clause 6 — Planning

### 6.1 Actions to address risks and opportunities

**Required.** Considering the issues (4.1) and the requirements (4.2), determine the risks and
opportunities that need to be addressed to: give assurance that the QMS can achieve its intended
results; enhance desirable effects; prevent or reduce undesired effects; achieve improvement.

Plan actions to address these risks and opportunities, how to integrate and implement them into the
QMS processes, and how to evaluate their effectiveness. **Actions must be proportionate to the
potential impact on the conformity of products and services** — the proportionality criterion to
apply.

Options for addressing risk (indicative): avoiding the risk, taking the risk to pursue an
opportunity, eliminating the source, changing the likelihood or consequences, sharing, or retaining
the risk based on an informed decision. Opportunities can lead to new practices, new products, new
markets, new customers, partnerships, new technology.

**No documented information is required here.** No register, no method, no scoring is imposed. The
organisation must nevertheless be able to demonstrate that it has determined risks and planned
actions.

**Evidence to look for.** A risk register or table if one exists; minutes where risks are discussed;
actions tracked in action plans or process sheets; effectiveness evaluation in the management review
(9.3.2 e).

**Frequent gaps.** Risks listed with no associated action. Actions not integrated into processes,
living in a parallel table. Effectiveness never evaluated — the most commonly missed requirement in
the whole clause 6. No link to the issues from 4.1. Conversely, on the auditor's side: demanding a
formal register or a scoring method, which the standard does not ask for.

### 6.2 Quality objectives and planning to achieve them

**Required.** Establish quality objectives at relevant functions, levels, and processes. They must be
consistent with the quality policy, **measurable**, take applicable requirements into account, be
relevant to the conformity of products and services and to enhancing customer satisfaction, be
monitored, communicated, and updated as appropriate. They are **maintained as documented
information**.

For each objective, determine: what will be done, the resources required, who will be responsible,
when it will be completed, and how the results will be evaluated — the five planning elements.

**Frequent gaps.** Objectives that aren't measurable ("improve quality"). Set only at company level,
never broken down to processes. One or more of the five planning elements missing, deadline and
evaluation method being the most commonly absent. Objectives unrelated to the policy. Never updated
once past their deadline.

### 6.3 Planning of changes

**Required.** When the need for a change to the QMS is determined, the change is carried out in a
planned manner, considering the purpose of the change and its potential consequences, the integrity
of the QMS, the availability of resources, and the allocation or reallocation of responsibilities and
authorities.

**Frequent gap.** An often-ignored clause. Reorganisations, tool or site changes carried out with no
impact analysis on the QMS whatsoever.

## Clause 7 — Support

### 7.1 Resources

**7.1.1 General** — Identify and provide the resources needed, considering the capabilities and
constraints of existing internal resources and what needs to be obtained from external providers.

**7.1.2 People** — Determine and provide the people necessary for the effective implementation of the
QMS and for the operation and control of its processes.

**7.1.3 Infrastructure** — Determine, provide, and maintain the infrastructure necessary: buildings
and associated utilities, equipment including hardware and software, transportation resources,
information and communication technology.

**7.1.4 Environment for the operation of processes** — Determine, provide, and maintain the
environment necessary. It combines human and physical factors: social (non-discriminatory, calm,
non-confrontational), psychological (stress reduction, burnout prevention, emotional protection),
physical (temperature, humidity, light, airflow, hygiene, noise). The psychosocial dimension is
explicitly in the standard and commonly forgotten.

**7.1.5 Monitoring and measuring resources** — Determine and provide the resources needed to ensure
valid and reliable results; ensure the resources are suitable for the specific type of activity and
are maintained to remain fit for purpose; **retain documented information** as evidence of fitness for
purpose.

*7.1.5.2 Measurement traceability* — Where required or considered essential, measuring equipment must
be calibrated and/or verified at specified intervals or before use, against measurement standards
traceable to international or national standards (retain the basis used as documented information if
no such standard exists); identified to determine its calibration status; safeguarded from
adjustments, damage, or deterioration that would invalidate the calibration. If equipment is found
unfit for purpose, determine whether the validity of previous results was adversely affected and take
appropriate action — the retrospective-challenge step, almost always missing.

**7.1.6 Organizational knowledge** — Determine the knowledge necessary for the operation of processes
and for achieving conformity of products and services; maintain it and make it available. When
addressing changing needs, determine how to acquire or access any necessary additional knowledge.
Internal sources: intellectual property, experience gained, lessons learned from failures and
successes, undocumented knowledge, results of improvements. External sources: standards, academia,
conferences, knowledge gained from customers and providers.

*Frequent gap:* a clause introduced in 2015, often treated as a synonym for training. It targets the
organisation's own knowledge, in particular the risk of losing knowledge when a key person leaves.

### 7.2 Competence

**Required.** Determine the necessary competence of people doing work, under the organisation's
control, that affects the performance and effectiveness of the QMS; ensure they are competent based
on appropriate education, training, or experience; where applicable, take actions to acquire the
necessary competence and **evaluate the effectiveness of the actions taken**; **retain documented
information** as evidence of competence.

**Frequent gaps.** Training-attendance certificates mistaken for evidence of competence. Effectiveness
of actions never evaluated. Scope limited to employees, when the requirement covers anyone working
under the organisation's control, including temp staff and on-site subcontractors.

### 7.3 Awareness

**Required.** Ensure that people doing work under the organisation's control are aware of the quality
policy, of relevant quality objectives, of their contribution to the effectiveness of the QMS
including the benefits of improved performance, and of the implications of not conforming with QMS
requirements.

**Evidence.** Verified by interview, not by an attendance sheet. The four points above are exactly the
four questions to ask.

### 7.4 Communication

**Required.** Determine the internal and external communications relevant to the QMS: on what, when,
with whom, how, and who communicates. All five dimensions are required; "who communicates" is the one
most often left out.

### 7.5 Documented information

**7.5.1** The QMS includes the documented information required by the standard and that which the
organisation determines is necessary for its effectiveness. Its extent can vary depending on the
organisation's size, its type of activities, processes, products, and services, the complexity of
processes and their interactions, and the competence of people.

**7.5.2 Creating and updating** — Ensure appropriate identification and description (title, date,
author, reference number), format (language, software version, graphics) and media (electronic,
paper), and review and approval for suitability and adequacy.

**7.5.3 Control** — Documented information must be controlled to ensure it is available and suitable
for use, where and when it is needed, and adequately protected (from loss of confidentiality, improper
use, loss of integrity). Address, as applicable: distribution, access, retrieval, and use; storage and
preservation, including preservation of legibility; control of changes, including version control;
retention and disposition.

Documented information of external origin determined to be necessary must be identified and
controlled. That retained as evidence of conformity must be protected from unintended alteration.

**Frequent gaps.** Obsolete documents still in circulation. Multiple versions with no identifiable
reference version. Externally originated documents (standards, customer requirements, regulations) not
controlled. Retention periods not defined. No protection of records against modification.

## Clause 8 — Operation

### 8.1 Operational planning and control

**Required.** Plan, implement, and control the processes needed to meet requirements and to
implement the actions from clause 6, by: determining the requirements for products and services;
establishing criteria for those processes and for the acceptance of products and services;
determining the resources needed; implementing control of the processes in accordance with the
criteria; determining, maintaining, and retaining documented information to the extent necessary to
have confidence that processes were carried out as planned and to demonstrate conformity.

Control planned changes, review the consequences of unintended changes, and act to mitigate any
adverse effects. Ensure that **outsourced processes** are controlled (see 8.4).

### 8.2 Requirements for products and services

**8.2.1 Customer communication** — Must include: providing information relating to products and
services; handling enquiries, contracts, orders, and amendments; obtaining customer feedback **on
products and services, including complaints**; handling or controlling customer property; establishing
specific requirements for contingency actions, when relevant.

**8.2.2 Determining the requirements** — Ensure requirements are defined, including applicable
statutory and regulatory requirements and those considered necessary by the organisation, and that the
organisation can meet claims for the products and services it offers.

**8.2.3 Review of the requirements** — Before committing, conduct a review covering: requirements
specified by the customer, including delivery and post-delivery activities; requirements not stated by
the customer but necessary for the specified or intended use, where known; requirements specified by
the organisation; statutory and regulatory requirements; contract or order requirements differing from
those previously expressed. Any such differences must be resolved. Customer requirements not
documented must be confirmed before acceptance. A formal review for every order is not always
practicable — for online sales, for instance, the review can cover product information, catalogues, or
promotional material.

**Retain documented information** on the results of the review and on any new requirement.

**8.2.4 Changes to requirements** — Amend the relevant documented information and make the relevant
personnel aware of the changed requirements.

*Frequent gaps:* orders accepted with no review of ability to deliver; implicit fitness-for-use
requirements never examined; verbal orders not confirmed; mid-contract changes accepted without
updating or informing the relevant teams.

### 8.3 Design and development of products and services

Applicable whenever the organisation designs — which includes software development, service design,
and process engineering, regularly excluded wrongly.

**8.3.2 Design and development planning** — Consider: the nature, duration, and complexity of the
activities; the required stages, including applicable reviews; the verification and validation
activities required; the responsibilities and authorities involved; internal and external resource
needs; the need to control interfaces between people involved; the need for involvement of customers
and users; subsequent provision requirements; the level of control expected by customers and other
interested parties; documented information needed to demonstrate requirements have been met.

**8.3.3 Design and development inputs** — Determine the requirements essential for the specific type
of products and services, considering functional and performance requirements, information derived
from previous similar design activities, statutory and regulatory requirements, standards or codes of
practice the organisation has committed to implement, and the potential consequences of failure.
Inputs must be adequate, complete, and unambiguous; conflicting inputs must be resolved. **Retain
documented information.**

**8.3.4 Design and development controls** — Ensure the results to be achieved are defined; that
**reviews** are conducted to evaluate the ability of the results to meet requirements; that
**verification** activities ensure outputs meet input requirements; that **validation** activities
ensure resulting products and services meet requirements for the specified application or intended
use; that necessary actions are taken on problems found; that documented information is retained.
Review, verification, and validation have distinct purposes and can be conducted separately or
combined — confusing verification with validation is a classic error.

**8.3.5 Design and development outputs** — Ensure they meet input requirements; are adequate for
subsequent processes; include or reference monitoring and measuring requirements and acceptance
criteria; specify the characteristics essential for the intended use and for proper and safe
provision. **Retain documented information.**

**8.3.6 Design and development changes** — Identify, review, and control changes made during, or
subsequent to, design and development, to prevent any adverse impact on conformity. **Retain
documented information** on the changes, the review results, the authorisation of changes, and the
actions taken to prevent adverse impacts.

### 8.4 Control of externally provided processes, products and services

**8.4.1** Applies in three cases: externally provided products and services intended for
incorporation into the organisation's own; those provided directly to the customer on the
organisation's behalf; a process or part of a process provided by an external provider as a result of
a decision by the organisation.

Determine and apply criteria for the **evaluation, selection, monitoring of performance, and
re-evaluation** of external providers, based on their ability to provide in accordance with
requirements. **Retain documented information** on these activities and on any actions arising from
the evaluations.

**8.4.2 Type and extent of control** — Ensure that externally provided processes, products, and
services do not adversely affect the ability to consistently deliver conforming products and
services. Ensure outsourced processes remain within the control of the QMS; define both the controls
applied to the external provider and those applied to the resulting output; consider the potential
impact of the externally provided processes, products, and services on the organisation's ability to
consistently meet customer and statutory/regulatory requirements, as well as the effectiveness of the
controls applied by the external provider itself; determine the verification or other activities
necessary.

**8.4.3 Information for external providers** — Ensure the adequacy of requirements before
communicating them. Communicate: the processes, products, and services to be provided; the approval of
products and services, methods, processes and equipment, and release; competence, including any
required qualification of persons; the interactions the external provider has with the organisation;
control and monitoring of the provider's performance to be applied by the organisation; verification
or validation activities that the organisation or its customer intends to perform at the external
provider's premises.

*Frequent gaps:* evaluation criteria that don't exist or are never applied; no periodic re-evaluation;
performance monitoring not tracked; outsourced processes treated as out of scope when they remain the
QMS's responsibility; service providers (IT, logistics, intellectual subcontracting) forgotten in
favour of material suppliers alone.

### 8.5 Production and service provision

**8.5.1 Control of production and service provision** — Controlled conditions including, as
applicable: the availability of documented information defining the characteristics and results to be
achieved; the availability and use of suitable monitoring and measuring resources; the implementation
of monitoring and measurement activities at appropriate stages to verify control and acceptance
criteria; suitable infrastructure and environment; the appointment of competent persons, including
required qualification; **validation, and periodic revalidation**, of the ability of processes whose
resulting output cannot be verified afterward (special processes); **actions to prevent human error**;
the implementation of release, delivery, and post-delivery activities.

*The requirement to prevent human error is new in 2015 and widely ignored: mistake-proofing, double
checks, automation, interface design.*

**8.5.2 Identification and traceability** — Identify outputs when necessary to ensure the conformity
of products and services; identify the **status of outputs with respect to monitoring and
measurement requirements** throughout production and service provision; control the unique
identification of outputs when traceability is required, and **retain the documented information**
needed.

**8.5.3 Property belonging to customers or external providers** — Identify, verify, protect, and
safeguard such property. If lost, damaged, or found unsuitable for use, report to the owner and
**retain documented information**. Property can be tangible, but also intangible: intellectual
property and **personal data**.

**8.5.4 Preservation** — Preserve outputs to the extent necessary to ensure conformity:
identification, handling, contamination control, packaging, storage, transmission or transportation,
protection.

**8.5.5 Post-delivery activities** — Consider statutory and regulatory requirements, potential
undesired consequences, the nature, use, and intended lifetime of products and services, customer
requirements, and customer feedback. Can include actions under warranty provisions, contractual
maintenance obligations, and recycling or final disposal.

**8.5.6 Control of changes** — Review and control changes for production or service provision to the
extent necessary to ensure continuing conformity. **Retain documented information** describing the
results of the review, the person(s) authorising the change, and any necessary actions arising from
the review.

### 8.6 Release of products and services

**Required.** Implement planned arrangements, at appropriate stages, to verify that requirements have
been met. Release must not proceed until planned arrangements have been satisfactorily completed,
unless approved by a relevant authority and, as applicable, by the customer. **Retain documented
information** including evidence of conformity with the acceptance criteria and traceability to the
person(s) authorising release.

*Frequent gap:* traceability of who authorised release is often missing — it's known that the check
happened, not who signed off on release.

### 8.7 Control of nonconforming outputs

**Required.** Identify and control outputs that do not conform to their requirements to prevent
their unintended use or delivery. Take action appropriate to the nature of the nonconformity and its
effect, including for nonconformities **detected after delivery of products, during, or after the
provision of services**. Deal with nonconforming outputs by one or more of the following: correction;
segregation, containment, return, or suspension of provision; informing the customer; obtaining
authorisation for acceptance under concession. Verify conformity after correction.

**Retain documented information** that describes the nonconformity, the actions taken, any
concessions obtained, and identifies the authority deciding the action.

*Frequent gaps:* the setup limited to physical products, when nonconforming services fall under the
same requirement; nonconformities detected after delivery not handled through this process; the
deciding authority not identified in the record.

## Clause 9 — Performance evaluation

### 9.1 Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation

**9.1.1** Determine what needs to be monitored and measured, the methods needed to ensure valid
results, when the monitoring and measuring must be performed, and when the results must be analysed
and evaluated. **Evaluate the performance and the effectiveness of the QMS.** Retain documented
information as evidence of the results.

**9.1.2 Customer satisfaction** — Monitor customers' **perceptions** of the degree to which their
needs and expectations have been fulfilled, and determine the methods for obtaining, monitoring, and
reviewing this information. Examples: surveys, feedback on delivered products and services, meetings
with customers, market-share analysis, compliments, warranty claims, dealer reports.

*Point of attention:* the requirement is about customer **perception**, not internal indicators. An
on-time delivery rate is not a measure of customer satisfaction — it's internal performance. This is
a classic gap.

**9.1.3 Analysis and evaluation** — Analyse and evaluate appropriate data and information arising from
monitoring and measurement. The results are used to evaluate: conformity of products and services;
the degree of customer satisfaction; the performance and effectiveness of the QMS; whether planning
has been implemented effectively; the effectiveness of actions taken to address risks and
opportunities; the performance of external providers; the need for improvements to the QMS. All seven
dimensions are required.

*Frequent gap:* data collected and displayed, but not **analysed or evaluated** — a dashboard is not
an analysis. Points (e) and (f) are the most often missing.

### 9.2 Internal audit

**Required.** Conduct internal audits at planned intervals to determine whether the QMS conforms to
the organisation's own requirements **and** to the requirements of the standard, and is effectively
implemented and maintained.

Plan, establish, implement, and maintain an audit programme(s) including the frequency, methods,
responsibilities, planning requirements, and reporting. The programme takes into account the
importance of the processes concerned, changes affecting the organisation, and the results of
previous audits. Define the audit criteria and scope for each audit. Select auditors and conduct
audits to ensure **objectivity and impartiality** of the audit process. Report results to relevant
management. Take correction and appropriate corrective actions **without undue delay**. **Retain
documented information** as evidence of the implementation of the audit programme and the audit
results.

ISO 19011 provides guidance — a recommendation, not a requirement.

*Frequent gaps:* a programme not based on the importance of the processes nor on prior results — every
process audited at the same frequency regardless of criticality; an auditor auditing their own work;
audits covering internal procedures without checking against the standard's requirements; findings
with no follow-up, or followed up long after the fact.

### 9.3 Management review

**9.3.1** Top management reviews the QMS, at planned intervals, to ensure its continuing
**suitability, adequacy, effectiveness, and alignment with the strategic direction**.

**9.3.2 Management review inputs** — The status of actions from previous management reviews; changes
in external and internal issues relevant to the QMS; information on the performance and effectiveness
of the QMS, including **trends** in customer satisfaction and feedback from relevant interested
parties, the extent to which quality objectives have been met, process performance and conformity of
products and services, nonconformities and corrective actions, monitoring and measurement results,
audit results, the performance of external providers; the adequacy of resources; the effectiveness of
actions taken to address risks and opportunities; opportunities for improvement.

**9.3.3 Management review outputs** — Decisions and actions related to opportunities for improvement,
any need for changes to the QMS, and resource needs. **Retain documented information** as evidence.

*Frequent gaps:* one or more input items missing — adequacy of resources and effectiveness of actions
addressing risk topping the list; data presented as a snapshot rather than as **trends**; a descriptive
report with no decisions or actions; follow-up from the previous review not done.

## Clause 10 — Improvement

### 10.1 General

**Required.** Determine and select opportunities for improvement and implement any necessary actions
to meet customer requirements and enhance customer satisfaction, including: improving products and
services to meet requirements as well as to address future needs and expectations; correcting,
preventing, or reducing undesired effects; improving the performance and effectiveness of the QMS.
Improvement can include correction, corrective action, continual improvement, breakthrough change,
innovation, and reorganisation.

### 10.2 Nonconformity and corrective action

**Required.** When a nonconformity occurs, **including any arising from a complaint**:

a) React: control and correct it, and deal with the consequences.
b) Evaluate the need for action to eliminate the cause(s) so that it does not recur or occur
elsewhere, by reviewing and analysing the nonconformity, determining its causes, and **determining if
similar nonconformities exist, or could potentially occur**.
c) Implement any action needed.
d) **Review the effectiveness** of any corrective action taken.
e) Update risks and opportunities determined during planning, if necessary.
f) Make changes to the QMS, if necessary.

Corrective actions must be appropriate to the effects of the nonconformities encountered.

**Retain documented information** as evidence of the nature of the nonconformities and any subsequent
actions taken, and of the results of any corrective action.

*Frequent gaps, in order of frequency:* a correction mistaken for a corrective action; effectiveness
never verified (point d); the search for similar cases never done (point b.3); risks and opportunities
not updated (point e); customer complaints handled commercially, outside the nonconformity process.

### 10.3 Continual improvement

**Required.** Continually improve the suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness of the QMS. Consider
the results of analysis and evaluation, and the outputs from management review, to determine whether
there are needs or opportunities to be addressed.

*Frequent gap:* no demonstration of improvement beyond handling nonconformities. Fixing gaps is
maintenance, not continual improvement.
