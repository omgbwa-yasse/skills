# Worked examples

## A report condensed (Standard)

Source: a 12-page market analysis report.

> **Our fastest-growing competitor threat is in the premium segment, not the mass market we've been
> watching.**
>
> - Competitor X's premium-tier revenue grew 34% year-over-year, more than triple the category
>   average, while our premium share fell from 22% to 18% over the same period.
> - Their growth is concentrated in three metro markets where we have minimal premium presence — not
>   a broad-based gain that would be harder to counter.
> - Our mass-market position is actually stable; the data initially flagged as concerning there
>   reflects a seasonal pattern consistent with the last three years, not a new trend.
>
> **This suggests reallocating the Q3 premium marketing budget toward the three metro markets named
> above, rather than the broader campaign currently planned.**
>
> Full analysis, methodology, and market-by-market breakdown: [source document].

Notice: the conclusion names the specific, non-obvious finding (premium segment, not mass market —
the opposite of what the team had been watching), not a generic "market analysis complete."

## A technical report condensed for non-technical leadership (Standard)

Source: an infrastructure incident post-mortem.

> **The outage was caused by a single point of failure we knew about but hadn't prioritised fixing —
> it will recur without the fix now scheduled for next sprint.**
>
> - The database failover mechanism had not been tested since a configuration change six months ago;
>   when the primary failed, the backup didn't activate as designed.
> - This was flagged as a risk in the Q1 infrastructure review but ranked below other priorities at
>   the time.
> - The fix (re-testing and monitoring the failover path) is scoped and now first in next sprint.
>
> **No further action needed from leadership beyond confirming the fix's priority holds against
> other sprint demands.**
>
> Full incident timeline and technical detail: [source document].

Notice: technical detail (which specific mechanism, what configuration) is present but subordinate —
enough for a non-technical reader to trust the explanation without needing to evaluate it themselves.

## Multi-source synthesis (Deep)

Source: three vendor proposals for the same contract.

> **All three vendors can meet the technical requirements; the real difference is delivery risk, and
> Vendor B is the strongest on that dimension despite not having the lowest price.**
>
> - Price: Vendor A is 12% below B and C, but A's proposal timeline assumes resource availability
>   they haven't confirmed — flagged as a risk in their own proposal's fine print.
> - Track record: B and C both have relevant delivery history; B's most recent comparable project
>   finished on schedule, C's ran four months over.
> - Support terms: converge across all three — not a meaningful differentiator this time, despite
>   being the focus of most of the proposal documents themselves.
>
> Sources agree on technical fit; they diverge mainly on how much weight to put on price versus
> delivery risk — this summary weights delivery risk higher given C's recent track record.
>
> Full proposals: [source documents A, B, C].
