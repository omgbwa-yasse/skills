# Alignment method, step by step

How to actually run the "align, don't cascade" process from SKILL.md across multiple teams — the
practical sequence, not just the principle.

## Step 1: leadership shares context, not objectives-to-copy

Before any team drafts anything, share: the results of the previous period (what moved, what
didn't), the reasoning behind the direction chosen for this period, and any known hard constraints
(a fixed deadline, a budget ceiling). Explicitly do not hand down pre-written objectives for teams to
adopt — that's the cascade this method avoids.

## Step 2: each team drafts independently

Teams write their own objectives and key results from the shared context, informed by what they
know about their own function that leadership doesn't see in detail. This is where local expertise
enters — a support team knows what's actually driving ticket volume better than a leadership team
does.

## Step 3: cross-check for connection, not conformity

Once drafts exist, check each team's objectives against the shared priorities with one question per
objective: "if this team fully achieves this, does it visibly help move one of the shared
priorities?" Three possible answers:

- **Yes, directly** — keep as drafted, note the connection in the alignment map.
- **Indirectly, through a chain** (e.g. a faster platform → faster product shipping → the company
  growth priority) — keep, but don't force the objective's own wording to mention the top-level
  priority; the chain lives in the map, not the objective text.
- **Not really** — this is the moment to ask whether it's a legitimate team-specific priority (fine,
  within the "minority team-specific" allowance) or whether the team drafted it because they weren't
  sure what else to write (worth a real conversation, not a rewrite imposed from above).

## Step 4: build the alignment map

A simple table, not a hierarchy diagram — hierarchy diagrams visually imply cascading even when the
underlying process didn't:

| Team objective | Connects to | Nature of connection |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------------------------|
| Platform: reduce response time | Company: growth | Indirect — faster platform enables faster product shipping |
| Support: reduce reopen rate | Company: retention | Direct |
| Design: refresh the component library | — | Team-specific, no forced link |

## Step 5: revisit mid-period, not just at grading

If a higher-level priority shifts mid-quarter (a market change, a funding change), the teams whose
OKRs connected to it need to hear about it explicitly and get the chance to adjust — see the
non-negotiable on this in SKILL.md. Build a mid-period check into the calendar rather than relying on
someone remembering to flag it.

## What "too many levels" looks like in practice

Organisation → division → team → individual is four levels. By the time an individual's OKR has been
derived through three translation steps, it typically bears little resemblance to anything a person
actually recognises as their own — check by asking the individual whether they could restate their
objective in their own words without looking at the document. If they can't, the cascade has gone one
level too far.
