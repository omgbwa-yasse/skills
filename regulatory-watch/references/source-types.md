# Source types by framework category

Where to look for sources per framework type, to make "identify sources per framework" in SKILL.md
concrete rather than a research task left entirely to the user.

## International standards (ISO, IEC, and equivalents)

- **Primary source:** the standards body itself (ISO, IEC) publishes revision schedules and
  committee drafts, though the standard text itself is usually paywalled.
- **Secondary sources:** national standards bodies (which often publish adoption timelines and
  summaries), certification bodies' newsletters, sector associations that track standard revisions
  relevant to their members.
- **Typical cadence:** standards revise on multi-year cycles (5+ years is common); the relevant
  cadence per SKILL.md's table is quarterly review of the revision cycle, not continuous monitoring.

## National and supranational legislation

- **Primary source:** official gazette or equivalent (the authoritative publication of enacted law),
  and the legislature's own tracking of bills in progress for anything not yet enacted.
- **Secondary sources:** law firms and industry associations frequently publish plain-language
  summaries faster than official channels update, useful for the "what changes" qualification step
  even when the primary source remains the authority cited.
- **Typical cadence:** near-real-time for enacted texts affecting active compliance work; weekly or
  bi-weekly for bills still in the legislative process.

## Sector-specific regulation (financial services, healthcare, food safety, data protection, etc.)

- **Primary source:** the sector regulator's own publications — most maintain a public register of
  guidance, enforcement actions, and consultations.
- **Secondary sources:** sector trade associations often run a dedicated regulatory-watch function
  and share it with members; worth confirming whether the organisation already has access to one
  before building a parallel one from scratch.
- **Typical cadence:** varies widely by sector activity level; a newly regulated area (e.g. AI
  governance in the current period) needs near-real-time attention even before anything is formally
  enacted, because organisations often need lead time to prepare.

## Contractual requirements from clients or principals

- **Primary source:** the contract itself, plus any amendment or side-letter process defined in it.
- **Secondary sources:** the relationship owner on the client side is often the fastest channel for
  an upcoming change, ahead of any formal amendment being issued.
- **Typical cadence:** tied to contract renewal cycles and any explicitly defined notice periods —
  check the contract's own amendment clause for the required notice window and calibrate the review
  cadence to arrive before it, not after.

## Internal policy frameworks (when the "framework" is the organisation's own standard, not external)

- **Primary source:** whoever owns the internal policy set — often the same function `smq-analysis`
  works with for QMS-internal documents.
- Worth distinguishing explicitly from an external framework in the scope definition: an internal
  policy the organisation controls doesn't need the same urgency calibration as an externally imposed
  one, since the organisation can plan its own policy changes rather than reacting to someone else's.
