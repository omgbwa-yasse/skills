# Status report structure

## Calibrate by audience

| Audience | Level of detail | What matters most |
|---|---|---|
| **Project team** | Activity by activity | Who does what this week, which blockers to clear |
| **Steering committee** | Milestone by milestone | Significant variances, decisions needed, budget |
| **Donor / client** | Results and contractual commitments | Compliance with promised deliverables and deadlines, justification of variances |

Never send a steering committee the level of detail of a team meeting: it drowns the decisions that
need making under operational detail and loses the thread of what matters to that audience.

## RAG framework (Red / Amber / Green)

Used as the opening summary of a report to a steering committee or a donor, before the detail.

| Status | Meaning | Switch-over criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | On plan | No missed milestone, budget spent proportional to progress, no blocker without an identified solution |
| **Amber** | Managed variance | At least one milestone late but a credible catch-up plan is in place, or budget slightly ahead of pro-rata with no overrun projected at completion |
| **Red** | Unmanaged variance | A contractual milestone threatened with no agreed solution, a projected budget overrun, or a blocker with no decision-maker or deadline |

Set these criteria once, at the start of tracking, and apply them consistently afterward without
adjusting them report to report — this prevents a status from appearing to improve artificially.

## Periodic report structure

1. **Overall status** — RAG plus one summary sentence, for the time-pressed reader.
2. **Progress since the last update** — milestones reached, compared to plan.
3. **Variances** — each with a diagnosed cause, downstream impact, chosen option (see
   `tracking-method.md`).
4. **Budget** — actual vs. planned at the same date, projection to completion if a variance is under
   way.
5. **Risks** — new risks identified since the last update, evolution of risks already being tracked.
   Point to `risk-analysis` if a structured analysis is needed beyond a simple update.
6. **Decisions requested** — the question(s) the audience must decide on, stated explicitly, not
   diluted in the text.
7. **Next step** — what's planned before the next check-in.

Section 6 must never be empty in a report to a steering committee: a committee that never receives a
decision to make eventually stops reading the reports.
