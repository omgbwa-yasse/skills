# Bullets and action verbs

## The formula

**Action verb + what was done + scope or result.**

The third element is what separates a résumé that gets an interview from one that gets skimmed. Scope is a number: how many, how much, how often, for whom, over what period. Result is what changed.

Not every bullet can carry a number. Aim for roughly half.

## Worked rewrites

| Duty statement | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| Responsible for social media | Grew the association's LinkedIn following from 400 to 3,100 over eighteen months |
| Handled customer inquiries | Resolved 60 to 80 customer inquiries daily by phone and email, in English and French |
| Worked on the migration project | Migrated 12,000 records to a new document management system, cutting retrieval time from 15 minutes to under 2 |
| Assisted with reporting | Automated the monthly reporting cycle, replacing two days of manual compilation with a scheduled export |
| Duties included staff training | Trained 25 administrative staff on the new records platform, which cut data entry errors in the following quarter |

The pattern in every case: name the thing, size it, and say what changed.

## Verb bank

**Leadership and coordination**
led, directed, supervised, coordinated, chaired, mentored, delegated, oversaw, mobilized, headed

**Analysis and research**
analyzed, assessed, audited, investigated, evaluated, diagnosed, modelled, forecast, benchmarked, surveyed

**Building and delivering**
built, developed, designed, implemented, deployed, launched, engineered, configured, integrated, produced

**Improving**
streamlined, automated, restructured, consolidated, standardized, reduced, accelerated, simplified, upgraded, resolved

**Communication**
drafted, presented, negotiated, briefed, advised, facilitated, translated, documented, published, represented

**Administration and operations**
administered, processed, scheduled, tracked, maintained, reconciled, catalogued, monitored, verified, coordinated

**Teaching and support**
trained, coached, instructed, guided, onboarded, supported, advised, tutored

**Finance and procurement**
budgeted, forecast, procured, allocated, audited, reconciled, controlled, negotiated

## French verb bank (infinitive)

French Canadian bullets open with the infinitive. The same functional groupings, with the forms that actually appear in Franco-Ontarian and Québec practice:

**Direction et coordination**
diriger, superviser, coordonner, encadrer, piloter, animer, déléguer, présider

**Analyse et recherche**
analyser, évaluer, auditer, diagnostiquer, examiner, modéliser, recenser

**Conception et réalisation**
concevoir, élaborer, développer, mettre en œuvre, déployer, implanter, produire, paramétrer

**Amélioration**
optimiser, automatiser, restructurer, normaliser, réduire, simplifier, rationaliser, résoudre

**Communication**
rédiger, présenter, négocier, conseiller, informer, traduire, documenter, représenter

**Administration**
administrer, traiter, planifier, assurer le suivi, tenir à jour, vérifier, classer, contrôler

**Formation et soutien**
former, accompagner, guider, appuyer, encadrer, sensibiliser

Two forms to watch. *Assurer* and *effectuer* are correct but weak when overused — they describe presence rather than contribution, and a résumé where a third of the bullets open with *assurer* says very little. *Participer à* has the same problem as "assisted with" in English: it minimizes the contribution and invites the question of who did the work.

## Anti-patterns

**"Responsible for"** — describes the job description rather than the person. Replace with a verb showing what was actually done.

**"Helped with" and "assisted in"** — minimizes the contribution and invites the question of who did the real work. Name the specific contribution instead.

**"Successfully"** — every bullet on a résumé is implicitly a success. The word adds length and nothing else.

**Verb repetition** — five consecutive bullets starting with "Managed" reads as one undifferentiated block. Vary the openings.

**Tense drift** — past tense for past roles, present tense for the current one. Be consistent within each entry.

**Jargon with no external meaning** — internal system names, employer-specific acronyms, and project codenames mean nothing to a screener. Translate into the language the industry uses.

**Uniform bullet length** — bullets that are all exactly two lines read as generated. Real ones vary.

**Stacked triples** — "planning, coordination and execution", "analysis, design and implementation". One or two of these is fine. Six is a tell that the writing is padding rather than saying something.
