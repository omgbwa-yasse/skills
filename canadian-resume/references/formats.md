# Résumé formats

Three formats are recognized in Canada. The choice is strategic, not cosmetic: it decides what the screener notices first and what they have to hunt for.

## Reverse chronological

Work history listed newest first, each entry carrying the employer, location, dates, and bullets.

**Use when:**
- The person is applying within their current field
- Career progression is visible and upward
- Employment history has no large unexplained gaps

**Avoid when:**
- The history shows frequent job changes or long gaps that are hard to explain
- The most recent job is the least relevant one and would dominate the top of the page
- The link between past work and the new target is hard to see

This is the default and the safest choice with conservative employers, including most public-sector recruiters.

## Functional (skills-based)

Achievements grouped under competency headings — for example "Data analysis", "Client service", "Team supervision" — each supported by evidence drawn from anywhere in the person's history. Employment history is reduced to a bare list of titles, employers, and dates near the bottom.

**Use when:**
- The person is entering the workforce or has little paid experience
- Past jobs are unrelated to each other or to the target
- Skills were built through volunteering, study, or personal projects rather than employment
- The person is transferring competencies into a new context

**Avoid when:**
- Career progression is the selling point — this format hides it
- Applying to a conservative employer or a government process where the screener is matching experience to specific qualifications

Be aware that experienced screeners read this format as an attempt to conceal something. It works best when the covering letter explains the transition directly.

Order the competency headings by relevance to the posting, most relevant first.

## Combination

A skills summary at the top, then the reverse chronological history below. The person gets to frame their competencies before the screener reads their job titles, without hiding the history.

**Use when:**
- Frequent employer changes within one field
- Deep experience in a domain spread across many employers
- A pivot where the target job is adjacent to, not identical with, current work
- In doubt

This is the most common format in practice and the one to reach for unless there is a reason to do otherwise.

## Concrete shapes

The templates issued by Collège La Cité and the Gouvernement du Québec settle what each format actually looks like on the page. Reproduce these silhouettes rather than inventing one.

The section names below are the Variant B set. Under Variant A the same silhouettes hold with the plain headings — Professional summary, Skills, Work experience, Education — and without the trailing references line. Format and variant are independent choices: any of the four formats can be produced under either variant.

**Reverse chronological, entry level** — the shape for students, recent graduates, and anyone whose education outweighs their work history:

```
Name / address / phone / email
Bilingue dans les deux langues officielles
Études            (credential, institution, city, dates; expected completion if in progress)
Compétences       (grouped by domain with a bold label)
Expériences professionnelles   (reverse chronological, 4-6 bullets each)
Références        (Variant B only)
```

**Reverse chronological, experienced** — skills move above education once the work history carries the argument:

```
Name / contact
Compétences or Sommaire des qualifications
Éducation / Formation      (including Autres formations for short courses)
Historique de travail
```

**Combination** — the most common shape in practice:

```
Name / contact
Sommaire des qualifications    (4-6 bulleted, verifiable statements)
Expérience de travail          (reverse chronological, full bullets)
Éducation
```

**Functional (skills-based)**:

```
Name / contact
Sommaire des qualifications
Éducation
Compétences        (grouped under 3-5 competency domains, each with its own bullets)
Historique d'emploi   (bare list: employer, city, dates only — no bullets)
```

The last line is what defines the format. The employment history is reduced to a list with no detail, because the detail has already been given under the competency headings. Repeating it below would double the length and undo the point of the format.

## Academic CV

A different document, not a variant of the above. Longer, no page cap, and organized around publications, grants, teaching, conference presentations, and supervision. Use it only for academic, research, and some clinical positions. Applying to a non-academic employer with an academic CV is a common and costly error for recently graduated researchers — the document reads as unfocused because it was written for a different audience.

## Federal public-service applications

Neither of the above governs on its own. The screening is driven by the essential qualifications listed in the poster, and the application usually asks the candidate to demonstrate each one in a separate text box. See `public-sector.md`.
