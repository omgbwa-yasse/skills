# Sources and how conflicts were resolved

## Primary sources

**Job Bank (Government of Canada), "How to write a good resume"**
jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/write-good-resume

Supplies: no photo; no personal details such as age, weight, height, marital status, religion, or political views; never a Social Insurance Number; five to seven bullets maximum per section; no references on the résumé; no reasons for leaving previous jobs; hobbies only when related to the position; emphasis on recent experience with anything over fifteen years cut or summarized; tailoring using the posting's own terminology; quantified achievements; avoidance of vague statements; no first-person pronouns outside the summary.

Job Bank also operates a free Resume Builder for account holders, which exports RTF for conversion to PDF or .docx.

**Gouvernement du Québec, "Rédiger son curriculum vitæ"**
quebec.ca/emploi/trouver-emploi-stage/conseils/preparer-candidature/rediger-curriculum-vitae

Supplies: one to two pages maximum; reverse chronological ordering; truthful content only; simple vocabulary and no negative constructions; clear sections with subheadings; no humour; the three-format taxonomy (chronological, functional, combination) with the conditions favouring each; five duties maximum per job; action verbs; references kept off the résumé but carried to the interview with permission obtained beforehand; supplementary sections for languages, computer skills with proficiency levels, professional qualifications and associations, and awards.

Québec also publishes downloadable chronological and skills-based templates.

**Collège La Cité résumé templates (Ottawa)**

Four French-language models — two reverse chronological, one combination, one skills-based — distributed to students at a Franco-Ontarian college in the National Capital Region.

Supply: the infinitive verb form in bullets; French semicolon punctuation of lists; the standard section names (Sommaire des qualifications, Compétences, Études, Autres formations, Expériences professionnelles, Historique de travail, Historique d'emploi); the bulleted impersonal form of the qualifications summary; bilingualism stated as a line under the contact block; explicit expected-graduation dates; "(suite)" continuation headings; and the concrete section ordering of each format.

These are teaching templates for students. Their structure and register are reliable; their bullets are not — every one is a duty statement with no quantification. Follow the shape, not the writing.

## Where the sources disagree

Every conflict below is resolved by the variant the person chooses, not by the skill deciding. `variants.md` holds the two rule sets; what follows records what each source actually says and why both positions are defensible.

**Personal interests.** Québec treats them as a way to reveal personality and stand out. Job Bank advises against them because of the judgments employers may make, allowing them only when related to the position. Kept under Variant B, omitted under Variant A. The case for omitting: no candidate has been rejected for leaving out a hobby, while a hobby can trigger an unconscious bias. The case for keeping: a francophone screener reading a short institutional résumé uses them to form a picture of the person.

**Full address.** Québec asks for the complete address. Job Bank does not require it, and pan-Canadian practice has moved to city and province, which avoids location-based screening and reduces exposure of personal data. Full address under Variant B, city and province under Variant A.

**Career objective.** Québec recommends a one or two sentence career objective. Contemporary pan-Canadian practice favours a professional summary describing what the candidate offers rather than what they want. Permitted under Variant B, not used under Variant A.

**Summary form.** Québec and the La Cité templates open with a bulleted, impersonal *Sommaire des qualifications*. English Canadian practice opens with a prose summary in which first person is acceptable. Bulleted under Variant B, prose under Variant A.

**French verb form.** The institutional templates use the infinitive throughout. Private-sector and technical résumés commonly use the past participle. Infinitive under Variant B; either, held consistently, under Variant A.

**Trailing references line.** The La Cité templates end with "Références disponibles sur demande". Job Bank calls the line redundant. Both are current practice in French Canada. Kept under Variant B, omitted under Variant A.

## Where both are silent

Applicant tracking systems, security clearance, official language profiles, and credential equivalency for internationally trained candidates are not covered by either source. The guidance on those points in `public-sector.md` and in the machine-readability section of SKILL.md reflects standard Canadian hiring practice rather than either published guide, and should be verified against a specific employer's process when the stakes are high.

## Currency

The Québec page was last updated in November 2024. Job Bank does not display a revision date. Both are stable guidance rather than fast-moving policy, but verify against the actual posting whenever a requirement in it contradicts anything here. The posting always wins.
