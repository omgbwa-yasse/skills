# Review checklist

Work through this in order when auditing an existing résumé. Report by severity, not by section. The person needs to know what would get them rejected before they hear about their font.

## Severity 1 — would cause rejection or legal exposure

- Photo, date of birth, age, marital status, dependants, religion, ethnicity, political affiliation, or health information present
- Social Insurance Number or any government identifier present
- Any claim that is false or unverifiable: inflated title, stretched dates, a credential not held
- A foreign credential presented as a Canadian equivalent it has not been assessed as
- Reference names and contact details printed on the résumé without the referees having agreed
- No target — the document is not aimed at any particular job
- Contact information missing, wrong, or unreachable

## Severity 2 — would fail screening

- Spelling or grammar errors, especially in the first ten lines
- No alignment with the posting: none of the employer's terminology appears
- Duties listed instead of accomplishments, with no quantification anywhere
- Buried relevance: the qualifying experience sits on page two under something irrelevant
- Longer than two pages without an academic or public-sector reason
- Graduation dates or employment dates missing
- Inconsistent date formats or an unexplained chronology
- Machine-hostile layout: tables, columns, text boxes, content in headers or footers, images
- Non-standard section headings a parser will not recognize
- Contradictions between sections, or between the résumé and the LinkedIn profile
- Mixed variants: a prose summary followed by institutional headings, a Variant A layout carrying a references line, or French bullets alternating between infinitive and past participle. Identify which variant the document is closest to and bring the outliers into line rather than rewriting the whole thing to the other one

## Severity 3 — weakens the document

- Generic summary made of stock phrases with no specific content
- Unverifiable character claims in the skills section
- "References available upon request" taking a line
- Fifteen-year-old roles described in the same detail as current ones
- More than seven bullets on a single role
- Every bullet the same length and grammatical shape
- Repeated opening verbs
- Irrelevant hobbies
- Employer names that mean nothing outside their home country, with no descriptor
- Skills listed that the target job has no use for

## Severity 4 — polish

- Inconsistent punctuation at the end of bullets
- Mixed straight and curly apostrophes, or mixed dash styles
- Uneven spacing between sections
- Decorative font, or more than two fonts
- Widow lines and awkward page breaks
- A filename like "resume_final_v3.docx" rather than "Firstname_Lastname_Resume.docx"

## How to deliver the review

Establish which variant the résumé is written to before judging it. A full street address and a trailing references line are correct under Variant B and only look like errors when measured against Variant A. Say which variant the document appears to follow, confirm with the person whether it matches their target, and audit against that one.

Lead with the three things that matter most, not with a list of forty items. Then give the full findings.

Rewrite two or three of the person's actual bullets rather than explaining the principle abstractly. "Quantify your achievements" is advice they have heard before and cannot act on; seeing their own sentence turned into a stronger one is something they can copy across the rest of the document.

Be honest about structural problems that a rewrite cannot fix. If the real issue is that the person is applying to roles they are not qualified for, or that their foreign credential needs an equivalency assessment, say so. That is more useful than a better verb.
