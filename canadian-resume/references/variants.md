# The two variants

The published Canadian sources do not agree with each other. Job Bank and the Gouvernement du Québec give contradictory instructions on address, summary form, hobbies, and the references line, and the Franco-Ontarian college templates add further divergences on verb form and punctuation.

These are not errors in one source or the other. They are two coherent doctrines addressed to different screeners. Producing a résumé means choosing one and applying it consistently, because a document that mixes them looks inconsistent to both audiences.

**Never choose silently.** Ask the person which variant they want before drafting, recommend one based on their target, and name the variant used when delivering the file.

## Variant A — Standard (Job Bank)

The pan-Canadian norm. Optimized for applicant tracking systems, private-sector recruiters, and federal screening boards. Works in English and in French.

- **Contact block:** city and province only. No street address, no postal code
- **Opening section:** professional summary, three to five lines of prose. First person acceptable here and nowhere else
- **No career objective.** What the candidate wants is not the opening argument
- **Hobbies and personal interests:** omitted unless directly relevant to the posting
- **References:** no line at all. Not the referees, not "available on request"
- **Bullets:** five to seven per role
- **Terminal punctuation on bullets:** none
- **Section headings:** the plain parseable set — Professional summary, Skills, Work experience, Education. In French: Sommaire, Compétences, Expérience de travail, Formation
- **French verb form:** past participle or infinitive, chosen once and held consistently. The past participle reads as more results-oriented and suits private-sector and technical applications
- **Page-break continuation:** no repeated headings

**Recommend this variant for:** federal, provincial, and municipal government; staffing and consulting firms; technology, finance, and engineering; any employer large enough to parse résumés automatically; any application submitted through an online portal; candidates applying across several provinces.

## Variant B — Québec institutional

The convention taught by Services Québec, the CEGEPs, and the Franco-Ontarian colleges, and expected by francophone institutional employers. Written for a human screener reading on paper.

- **Contact block:** full address including postal code
- **Opening section:** **Sommaire des qualifications** — four to six bulleted statements, impersonal, third person. *Détient une maîtrise en...*, *Plus de 12 ans d'expérience en...*, *Bilingue dans les deux langues officielles*. Never *Je détiens*
- **Career objective:** permitted as one or two sentences, either replacing the sommaire or following it
- **Hobbies and personal interests:** permitted. Québec treats them as a way to show personality and stand out
- **References:** the line *Références disponibles sur demande* or *Seront fournies sur demande* closes the document
- **Bullets:** maximum five duties per role
- **Terminal punctuation:** semicolon after each item with a non-breaking space before it, period on the last
- **Section headings:** the institutional set — Sommaire des qualifications, Compétences, Études or Formation, Autres formations, Expériences professionnelles, Historique de travail, Historique d'emploi, Références. Set them in caps or bold with a rule running to the right margin
- **French verb form:** infinitive, always. *Accueillir*, *Coordonner*, *Élaborer*. Never the past participle
- **Page-break continuation:** repeat the heading with *(suite)*

**Recommend this variant for:** employers in Quebec; francophone institutions anywhere in Canada, including school boards, colleges, health networks, and community organizations; applications made through a college or CEGEP career service or an employment counsellor; candidates told by an intermediary to follow the Québec or La Cité model; paper submissions and in-person drop-offs.

## Where the variants agree

Everything not listed above. Both forbid the photo, date of birth, marital status, religion, political affiliation, and Social Insurance Number. Both cap the résumé at one to two pages, order experience newest first, require truthful content, expect action verbs, and expect the résumé to be tailored to the posting. Both keep referee names off the document.

The quantification rule — scope or result in roughly half the bullets — applies to both. Neither published source demands it, and the institutional templates ignore it entirely, but it is what separates a résumé that gets an interview from one that gets skimmed. Apply it under either variant.

## Divergence table

| Rule | A — Standard | B — Québec institutional |
|---|---|---|
| Address | City, province | Full address with postal code |
| Opening section | Prose summary, 3-5 lines | Bulleted sommaire, 4-6 items |
| Person | First person allowed in summary | Impersonal throughout |
| Career objective | Not used | Permitted |
| Hobbies | Only if job-relevant | Permitted |
| References line | Omitted | Kept |
| Bullets per role | 5-7 | 5 maximum |
| Bullet punctuation | None | Semicolons, final period |
| French verbs | Past participle or infinitive, held consistently | Infinitive |
| Headings | Plain, parser-friendly | Institutional set, ruled |
| Page continuation | None | *(suite)* |

## How to ask

Put the choice to the person in one question, with a recommendation attached rather than a bare menu. Something along these lines:

> Two conventions apply here and they contradict each other on a handful of points. Given that you're targeting [employer type], I'd suggest **[variant]** — it means [the two or three differences that will actually be visible to them]. Want that, or the other one?

If the person has no preference or says to just pick, use Variant A and say so in the delivery note. It is the safer default: it survives automated parsing, and no screener has ever rejected a résumé for omitting a hobby.

## Overrides

The variant sets defaults, not a cage. If the person wants Variant A but insists on keeping the references line, or Variant B without the full street address, honour it. Record the deviation in the delivery note so they know what was changed and can defend it.

One combination to warn against: Variant A headings with Variant B punctuation, or an infinitive summary followed by past-participle bullets. Mixed forms within one document read as carelessness under either doctrine. Consistency matters more than which variant was chosen.

## Producing both

When a candidate is applying to both a federal department and a francophone college, two files are the right answer, not a compromise. Offer this rather than trying to satisfy both doctrines in one document.
