# Risks and performance indicators

Used at Stage 4, Block F. This block can change the methodology: allow for the feedback loop before
costing.

---

## PART 1 — PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

The chain is built in three steps: **metrics → operational KPIs → project KPI**.

### 1.1 Metrics

The measurable elements of an action while it is being carried out. To find them: isolate one
activity and list everything countable within it.

*Example — bagging food parcels*: parcels assembled · parcels still to assemble · defective parcels ·
parcels transferred · parcels storable on site · working time per parcel.

*Other examples*: clearing a field → hectares cleared, working time, staff per activity, tractors
mobilised, daily catering cost. Training → people trained, attendance rate, assessment score.

**Filtering rule**: an activity may yield ten metrics; keep only those that genuinely inform
**quality**, **cost** or **lead time**. Check for each that a reliable data source exists — a metric
with no collection source is unusable.

### 1.2 Operational KPIs

The metric is compared against its target value. The most common form is a proportion.

| Metric | Operational KPI |
|--------|-----------------|
| Parcels assembled | Percentage of parcels assembled |
| Defective parcels | Percentage of defective parcels |
| Working time per parcel | Average time per parcel |

### 1.3 Project KPI

Aggregation of the operational KPIs. Two methods:

1. **Simple average** of completion rates — acceptable when activities carry comparable weight.
2. **Weighted average** — recommended. The coefficient reflects the real weight of the activity:
   cost, duration, or any other relevant parameter (feasibility, impact on quality).

*Example — building a classroom*: % foundation complete · % walls raised · % roof frame installed ·
% interior finishing → overall indicator weighted by the cost of each work package.

### 1.4 Verification sources

Every indicator must be checkable at any time by a supervisor.

| # | Indicator | Target value | Verification source |
|---|-----------|--------------|---------------------|
| 01 | Percentage registered | 100% | Registration form |
| 02 | Attendance rate | ≥ 90% | Attendance sheet |
| 03 | Pass rate | ≥ 80% | Assessment results |

**What KPIs are for**: during execution they allow schedule and budget drift to be projected and
corrected in time; at closure they ground the evaluation.

## PART 2 — PREVENTIVE RISK ANALYSIS

A method for identifying risks, defining measures and qualifying activities. It does not seek to
solve problems that have occurred, but to **imagine the most likely ones and immobilise them**.

### 2.1 Step 1 — Break the activity into elementary tasks

Isolate each action, activity or task unambiguously. Reuse the breakdown from section 17
(methodology) directly.

### 2.2 Step 2 — Identify and rate the risks of each task

Question: what risks could arise in carrying out this task?

- Generate **without filtering** first — no idea is bad at this stage.
- Then rate each risk by its degree of harm: **H** (high), **M** (medium), **L** (low). Risks do not carry equal weight and do not warrant equal rigour.

### 2.3 Step 3 — Define the measures

For each risk: "what must be done or put in place so that this risk does not materialise?" A measure
is a concrete action, not an intention.

### 2.4 Step 4 — Qualify the measures

Attach each measure to its family of origin. Six families:

| Family | Scope |
|--------|-------|
| **Objectives** | Of the activity itself |
| **Means** | Material, human, financial |
| **Information system** | Information management and flow |
| **Organisation** | Allocation of roles |
| **Methods and procedures** | Execution steps of the activity |
| **Supervision** | Coordination and control of the activity |

This qualification enables control throughout the activity and reveals imbalances: measures
concentrated in a single family signal a structural weakness.

### 2.5 Reporting table

| Elementary task | Attached risks | Rating | Specific measures | Qualification |
|-----------------|----------------|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| The task analysed | All associated risks | H / M / L | The step preventing occurrence | Family of the measure |

### 2.6 Feedback loop — mandatory

Once the analysis is done, **fold the retained measures back into section 17 (methodology)** before
costing. Tell the user which activities were added or changed, since they may affect resources,
duration and cost.

A preventive risk analysis that changes nothing in the methodology has served no purpose.

### 2.7 Other tools worth mentioning

For technically demanding projects, mention HAZOP and the bow-tie method to the user, along with the
catalogue of risk analysis tools in ISO 31010.

## PART 3 — EXTERNAL RISKS (section 20)

Strict discrimination from the preventive analysis:

| Origin of the risk | Section |
|--------------------|---------|
| Depends on method, resources or planning | **19 — Preventive risk analysis** |
| Depends on an actor outside the project | **20 — External risks** |

An internal risk parked in section 20 signals either a defective preventive analysis or insufficient
expertise on the activity — raise it with the user rather than letting it pass.

**Treatment**: for each external risk, state the chosen approach — shared with the party concerned,
transferred, or accepted — and make it known to all parties. That is precisely what the section is
for: making visible what the project team does not control.
