# Zero-based budgeting: the REBUILD framework

A structured way to run the "start from zero" step in SKILL.md, rather than applying it as a vague
instruction. Adapt the depth to Standard vs. Deep mode — a Rapid single-line justification doesn't
need this.

## R — Reset

Drop last year's number as the default starting point. The blank page is deliberate: the previous
year's figure carries no privileged claim on this year's budget just because it existed.

## E — Explain

For every discretionary line, write the outcome it funds before the amount — this is already a
non-negotiable in SKILL.md, and it's the step most often skipped when people say they're "doing ZBB"
but are really just trimming percentages off last year's total.

## B — Break into decision units

Group spending into units that can be individually evaluated and, if necessary, individually cut —
a team, a programme, an initiative, a vendor contract. A decision unit should be small enough that
cutting it is a meaningful, specific choice, not so small that the exercise becomes unmanageable
(a decision unit of one line item each defeats the purpose; a decision unit of "the whole
department" is too coarse to arbitrate).

## U — Understand drivers

For each decision unit, identify what actually drives its cost — headcount, transaction volume,
contract terms, seasonal demand. This matters for reforecasting later: a line driven by headcount
scales differently under a hiring freeze than a line driven by transaction volume under a demand
spike.

## I — Invest deliberately

Once decision units are understood, fund the best-return ones first rather than spreading available
budget evenly. This is where the actual arbitration happens — the previous four steps just make it
possible to arbitrate on real information instead of habit.

## L — Limit noise

Standardise the granularity of lines and the thresholds for what needs a full ZBB pass versus a
lighter review. Applying full zero-basing to every line, every year, creates a justification burden
disproportionate to the value — reserve it for discretionary spend, and do it periodically (e.g. a
rotating subset of decision units each year) rather than exhaustively every cycle.

## D — Deploy and monitor

Turn the finished plan into the actual monthly tracking decisions covered in SKILL.md's "Tracking
execution" section — REBUILD produces the budget, the tracking discipline keeps it honest afterward.

## When full ZBB is overkill

For a small team budget or a Rapid justification, running all seven steps is disproportionate. Take
just E (explain the outcome) and I (fund the best returns first) — the two steps that catch the most
common failure (spending on habit, not on priority) without the full apparatus.
