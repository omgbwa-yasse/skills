# Meeting preparation and facilitation

A successful meeting is decided before it starts. The most frequent failure is not poor
facilitation but the absence of a clear objective per agenda item: a meeting without an objective
per topic reliably drifts into endless discussion, because no one knows when a point is closed.

## Before anything else: pick the depth

Match effort to what the meeting is for. Three modes:

| Mode | When | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Rapid** | A recurring team sync, a 1:1, a short check-in with a known group | A bullet-list agenda with rough timing, in the conversation. No formal decision log unless something is actually decided. |
| **Standard** | A cross-functional meeting, a decision meeting, a first-time working group | Full agenda table with objective per item, pre-read sent ahead, live decision and action log. |
| **Deep** | A steering committee, a meeting with external parties, or one meant to produce a record others will rely on later (a governance body, a formal review) | Standard plus explicit ground rules stated at open, named minute-taker distinct from the facilitator, and a circulated log within 24 hours with sign-off if the setting requires it. |

For a Rapid meeting, do not force the full agenda table below — a bullet list with timing does the
job. Reserve the full structure for Standard and Deep.

## Before building the agenda

1. **The type of meeting.** Inform, decide, produce together (workshop), solve a problem. Each has a
   different format — a decision meeting that slides into a brainstorming workshop achieves nothing.
2. **Is this meeting actually necessary?** If the objective is purely to inform, a written message
   often works better. Tell the user so if that is the case, rather than preparing an unnecessary
   meeting.
3. **Who needs to be there, and why each of them.** Someone present with no role on any agenda item
   should not be invited — or their role should be clarified before the invitation goes out.

## Building the agenda

For each item: **title, objective (inform / decide / produce), owner, time allotted.** An agenda
without an objective per item is not an agenda — it is a list of topics.

| Item | Objective | Owner | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|

`references/meeting-types.md` details what actually differs between inform, decide, produce, and
problem-solving formats — read it when unsure which one an agenda item is.

Send the agenda ahead of time, with the documents needed to prepare — a decision item should never
have participants discovering the data during the meeting itself: that turns the meeting into a
collective reading session.

Allow more time than the natural estimate for decision items (discussion always takes longer than
expected) and less for information items (they can be sped up if the group already has the
information).

## During the meeting

**Open by restating the objective and the ground rules.** An opening that sets the frame in 1-2
minutes prevents later drift — restate what needs to come out of the meeting, not just the topic.

**Restate before deciding.** On a decision item, restate the chosen option out loud before logging
it — this immediately surfaces disagreements that were never voiced.

**Manage participation actively.** Explicitly invite silent voices on items that concern them;
tactfully redirect whoever is dominating by pointing to the timing ("let's park that, I'll note it
and come back if time allows").

**Distinguish discussion from decision.** An item may need a period of exchange followed by an
explicit close ("decided: we go with X"). Do not let an item close simply because time ran out
rather than because a real decision was made — this is the most frequent cause of a topic coming
back unchanged at the next meeting.

`references/facilitation-techniques.md` has scripted moves for each of the above — managing a
dominant voice, drawing out silence, closing a split discussion, recovering from scope creep, and
running a hybrid meeting.

## Decision and action log

Kept live, not reconstructed afterwards:

| Decision / action | Who | Deadline | Status |
|---|---|---|---|

Distinguish a **decision** (settled, not reopened absent new facts) from an **action** (a task to
execute following the decision). Circulate this log within 24 hours of the meeting — minutes that
arrive a week later have lost their commitment effect.

## The special case of the 1:1

A recurring manager-report 1:1 is not a status meeting and the agenda logic above only partly
applies. Two adjustments: let the report lead the agenda — their topics first, manager updates last,
so the time does not get consumed by reporting upward before anything else is discussed — and close
every session with a standing question in both directions, "what's one piece of feedback you have
for me?", so feedback becomes a habit exchanged in both directions rather than a once-a-year event.
Weekly or biweekly, 30 minutes, is the cadence that keeps a 1:1 useful without becoming a burden.

## The non-negotiables

**Every decision item ends with a decision actually made**, not "let's revisit this" with no
deadline — otherwise the item returns unchanged next time.

**Every action has a named owner and a deadline**, never "the team will look into it."

**Allotted time is either respected or the overrun is made explicit.** If an item runs over its
slot, say so out loud and let the group choose: continue by cutting a later item, or defer. Letting
an overrun silently eat into later items is the most common form of drift.

## Output

- **In the conversation** — agenda, facilitation plan, in-the-moment redirection advice.
- **`.md`** — agenda to circulate, decision log to keep and maintain.

`assets/table-templates.md` has ready-to-copy tables for the agenda, decision log, and 1:1 format.

Whatever the format, a Standard or Deep meeting's output always contains: an objective per item, a
named owner per item, and a decision log distinguishing decisions from actions.

## Working with what the user gives you

**They ask for help mid-meeting, in the moment** ("this is going nowhere, what do I do"). Skip the
agenda-building steps entirely — diagnose which non-negotiable is failing right now (no objective
stated, discussion never closed, time silently overrun) and give the one redirection line that fixes
it.

**They give you a rough list of topics with no timing or objectives.** Don't just add a time column —
ask, for each topic, whether it's meant to inform, decide, or produce something, since that
classification is what the rest of the structure depends on. Infer where it's obvious rather than
asking about every line.

**They want a recurring meeting redesigned**, not a single agenda. Diagnose the recurring failure
first (usually: no objective per item, or decisions never actually get closed) rather than producing
one more one-off agenda that will drift the same way next time.

## A note on limits

This skill structures the meeting and its record; it does not have authority over what gets decided
in it, and a well-run process does not guarantee a good decision. Where a meeting's purpose is
governance or compliance-sensitive (a formal board, a statutory committee), say so once and flag that
the organisation's own governance rules on quorum, minute-taking, and sign-off may impose requirements
beyond this skill's general practice.
