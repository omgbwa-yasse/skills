# Facilitation techniques

Concrete moves for the situations SKILL.md names but doesn't script.

## Managing a dominant voice

Don't confront directly in the moment — it reads as a public correction and makes the person
defensive, which produces more talking, not less. Instead:

- **Time-box redirection**: "Good point — let's park that and I'll come back to you if we have time,
  I want to hear from [name] on this too."
- **Structural prevention**: for a topic you know is likely to draw a dominant voice, use a round-
  robin ("let's go around, 30 seconds each") before opening to free discussion — it guarantees every
  voice is heard before the loudest one sets the frame.
- **Afterward, privately**: if the pattern repeats meeting after meeting, address it one-on-one, not
  in the room.

## Drawing out a silent voice

Silence in a meeting is not the same as agreement or lack of opinion — it's frequently seniority
dynamics, conflict-avoidance, or simply not having found a gap to speak into.

- Ask by name, with a specific and answerable question: "[Name], you've worked on this before — does
  this match what you saw?" rather than an open "does anyone have thoughts?" which the same few
  people will fill.
- If a topic clearly concerns someone's area but they haven't spoken, name it directly: "this affects
  your team specifically — what's your read?"

## Closing a discussion that's genuinely split

When the room doesn't converge naturally:

- **State the two positions back**, in neutral language, to confirm you've understood the actual
  disagreement (often it's smaller than it sounds once restated).
- **Name who has the authority to decide** if consensus isn't required — a group discussion doesn't
  always need unanimous agreement to close; sometimes it needs the input surfaced and then a single
  owner to decide.
- **If it must be resolved now**, use a quick structured method: dot-voting on options, or a
  straw poll to check where the room actually stands before spending more time debating a gap that
  might not be as wide as the loudest voices suggest.
- **If it can wait**, say so explicitly and set a real deadline for the decision rather than letting
  it dissolve into "let's think about it."

## Recovering from scope creep mid-meeting

A meeting drifts when a tangential but interesting point hijacks the agenda. The fix is procedural,
not personal:

- Name it out loud without judgement: "this is a good conversation, it's not what we scoped for
  today — let's capture it and decide whether it needs its own meeting."
- Log it in the decision/action log as "to be scheduled" so it doesn't feel dismissed, just deferred.

## Running a hybrid (in-person + remote) meeting without losing remote participants

- Default to the medium that includes everyone: if anyone is remote, run the whole meeting as if
  everyone were — shared screen, chat monitored for questions, camera on for the facilitator at
  minimum.
- Explicitly invite remote voices at natural pause points, since they can't jump in over cross-talk
  the way an in-person participant can.
