# Meeting types in detail

The three formats named in SKILL.md, expanded with what actually differs between them.

## Inform

Purpose: transfer information the audience needs, with no decision required from them in the room.

- Agenda item objective: state the fact or update, take brief clarifying questions, move on.
- Time discipline: the tightest of the three — a status update that runs long is usually solvable by
  sending it as a written note instead of a meeting.
- Facilitation failure to watch for: someone tries to turn an inform item into a decide item mid-
  meeting ("well while we're on this, should we also..."). Redirect it to its own agenda slot or a
  separate meeting rather than letting scope creep consume the time budget.
- When to skip the meeting entirely: if there are no questions expected and no discussion value, a
  written update reaches people on their own time and doesn't cost a room full of people their
  attention simultaneously.

## Decide

Purpose: reach and log a specific decision, with an owner and a deadline.

- Agenda item objective: state the question to be decided (mirroring the discipline of
  `decision-memo`), present the options if not already circulated, discuss, close with an explicit
  decision.
- Pre-read matters most here: a decision item where the data appears live in the room forces the
  group to read and think simultaneously, which produces worse decisions than one where people
  arrive having already formed a view.
- Facilitation failure to watch for: the meeting runs out of time and the item closes on "let's
  revisit next time" — see the non-negotiable in SKILL.md. If a decision item is genuinely too complex
  to close in the allotted time, that's a sign it needed a decision memo circulated beforehand, not
  more meeting time.

## Produce (workshop)

Purpose: create something together — a plan, a set of options, a shared understanding — that
wouldn't exist as well if built by one person alone and reviewed.

- Agenda item objective: state what artefact should exist by the end (a list, a draft, a prioritised
  set), not just a topic to discuss.
- Needs more structure than the other two, not less: a workshop with no facilitation technique
  (brainstorm-then-cluster, silent writing before discussion, dot-voting) degenerates into whoever
  talks most setting the direction.
- Facilitation failure to watch for: mistaking a workshop for a decide meeting and trying to close
  with a firm decision before the group has actually converged — a workshop's output is often a
  narrowed set of options, with the actual decision made afterward by whoever has that authority.

## Solve a problem

A fourth pattern worth naming separately from "produce": the meeting exists because something is
broken and the group needs to find the cause before anyone can decide what to do about it.

- Structure: state the problem factually (what's observed, since when, impact), spend the bulk of
  the time on cause analysis (a quick 5-whys or fishbone works in a meeting setting), only then move
  to options — deciding a fix before agreeing on the cause is the most common failure of this format.
- This format often produces a decide item as its output, not a decision itself — "we agree the cause
  is X, someone should now bring options back next week" is a legitimate close.
