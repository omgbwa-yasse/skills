# Templates

## Quality policy

- Context: why this commitment, in one sentence tied to the organisation's mission.
- Leadership commitments (2-4 lines): customer focus, continual improvement, regulatory compliance —
  the ones that are actually kept, not a generic list.
- Framework for quality objectives: how the policy breaks down into measurable objectives.
- Communication and review arrangements.

Keep it to one page. A long quality policy doesn't get read.

## Quality objectives (table)

| Objective | Indicator | Target | Deadline | Owner | Resources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Every objective must be measurable and consistent with the policy — not a general aspiration
("improve quality") but a target ("cut customer complaints by 20% over the year").

```
             Pilot / Indicators
```


## Work instruction

- Precise title: the exact task, not the umbrella process.
- Prerequisites: equipment, qualification, information needed before starting.
- Numbered steps, one action per step, verb in the infinitive.
- Warnings flagged explicitly (safety, quality, common error).
- Expected result: what a job well done looks like.

## Quality manual (if explicitly requested)

Produce only on explicit request — not required since ISO 9001:2015.

1. Presentation of the organisation and the QMS scope, with justification of any exclusions.
2. Process map (overall diagram, pointer to detailed process sheets).
3. Quality policy and objectives (pointer or reproduction).
4. How it connects to the standard's requirements, clause by clause, if the certifier or client
   requires it in that form.
5. References to the system's procedures and documented information.

The quality manual points to the detailed documents rather than reproducing them — it stays a summary
document, not a duplicate of the document-control system.
