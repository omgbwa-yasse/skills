# Proportionality

ISO 9001:2015 (clause 7.5.1, note) is explicit: the extent of documented information can differ
depending on the organisation's size, the type of activity, the complexity of processes, and the
competence of people. Systematically proposing the heaviest format is an analytical error, not
evidence of rigour.

## Two contrasting examples, same activity: handling a customer complaint

**A 5-person structure, a simple activity.**
Half a page. Who receives the complaint, where it's logged (a shared file is enough as a record),
response time, who decides on the commercial gesture. No flowchart needed: the sequence is linear and
everyone already knows it.

**An industrial group, multi-site activity.**
A full procedure with a flowchart, a severity-based decision matrix, an interface with the CRM
system, automatic escalation past a deadline, indicators consolidated by site, monthly review. The
same object — handling a complaint — justifies a considerably more elaborate setup because the
volume, the number of actors, and the risk of lost information demand it.

## Signals for calibrating the level

Move toward **more formality** when: the activity spans several departments or sites, turnover is
high (the document replaces collective memory), the activity is regulated or subject to third-party
certification, the consequences of an error are significant.

Move toward **less formality** when: a single person performs the activity and knows it by heart,
volume is low, the organisation is small with effective informal communication, added formality would
reduce no real risk.

## What proportionality does not justify

It never exempts an organisation from the records explicitly required by the standard (management
review outputs, evidence of competence, internal audit results, etc. — see the full list in
`smq-analysis/references/documented-information.md`). It applies to *how* to document, not *whether*
to document.
