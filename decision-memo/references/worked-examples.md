# Worked examples

One example per mode, annotated. Use these to calibrate length and tone, not to copy verbatim.

---

## Rapid — half a page

> **Should we move the Tuesday team sync to Wednesday?**
> Recommendation: yes, move it. Two of five members have a recurring conflict on Tuesdays; Wednesday
> has none. No cost, no dependency. Owner: team lead, effective next week.

Notice what's absent: no options table, no criteria section. The question was small enough that the
recommendation and its one-line reason carry the whole memo.

## Standard — full structure

> **Should we migrate from Vendor A to Vendor B for invoicing by Q3?**
>
> Our current invoicing errors have cost three client relationships this year, and Vendor A has not
> shipped the fix they promised in January. This memo recommends migrating to Vendor B.
>
> **Recommendation:** Migrate to Vendor B by end of Q3. It costs 15% more annually but eliminates the
> reconciliation step that has caused every error traced this year.
>
> | Option | Cost | Benefit | Main risks | Status |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | A — stay with current vendor | No change | No migration effort | Errors continue; Vendor A's fix
> has slipped twice already | Discarded — no credible timeline for the fix |
> | B — migrate to Vendor B | +15%/year, one-time migration cost | Eliminates the reconciliation step | 6-week migration, staff retraining | **Recommended** |
> | C — build in-house | Engineering time, ongoing maintenance | Full control | 6+ months, pulls
> engineering off roadmap | Discarded — cost and timeline disproportionate to the problem |
>
> **Criteria used:** error elimination (primary), cost, implementation timeline, ongoing maintenance
> burden.
>
> **Commitment:** Finance lead owns the migration; contract signed by August 15; go-live before
> September 30. If not decided by August 1, we miss the Q3 window and carry the current error rate
> another two quarters.

Notice the shape: recommendation before the table, options genuinely compared (including "do
nothing"), criteria named so the recommendation doesn't look arbitrary, and a commitment block with a
real consequence for delay.

## Deep — with dissent section

Same structure as Standard, plus, after the options table:

> **Dissent noted.** The support team lead flagged that Vendor B's support hours don't cover our
> after-hours incident window. Mitigation: a 3-month bridge contract with our current on-call
> provider, cost included in the 15% figure above. If this mitigation isn't acceptable to
> [stakeholder], the recommendation should be revisited before signing.

A Deep memo doesn't hide disagreement to look more decisive — it names it and shows it was addressed,
which is what makes the recommendation defensible to a board or committee that will ask "did anyone
push back on this?"
