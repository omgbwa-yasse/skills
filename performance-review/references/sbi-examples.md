# SBI worked examples

Contrasted pairs — the vague version and its SBI correction — across common feedback themes.

## "Not proactive enough"

**Vague:** "You need to be more proactive."

**SBI:** "In the last three sprint planning sessions [situation], you waited for tasks to be
assigned rather than flagging the two areas you'd identified as at-risk beforehand [behaviour]. We
lost a day catching up on one of them after it surfaced mid-sprint [impact]. Going forward, flag
risks as soon as you see them, even before they're confirmed [next step]."

## "Great team player"

**Vague:** "You're a great team player, keep it up."

**SBI:** "When Maria was out sick during the release last month [situation], you picked up her
review queue without being asked and flagged it in standup so no one duplicated work [behaviour].
The release shipped on time because of it [impact]. That instinct to cover a gap without waiting to
be asked is exactly what I want to see more of [next step]."

Notice: even positive feedback is stronger with SBI — "great team player" is pleasant but not
memorable or repeatable; the specific instance is both.

## "Poor communication"

**Vague:** "Your communication needs work."

**SBI:** "In the client call on the 14th [situation], the timeline update was given without
mentioning the two dependencies that could push it — the client found out about them from a
different channel a week later [behaviour], which made us look like we weren't tracking the project
closely [impact]. When there's a risk to a date, mention it even if it's not certain yet — clients
handle 'this might slip' much better than surprises [next step]."

## "Doesn't take feedback well"

This one is a character judgement in disguise — rewrite it around the specific interaction that
prompted the label.

**Vague:** "He gets defensive about feedback."

**SBI:** "When I raised the formatting issue in the report last week [situation], the response was
to explain why the original approach was fine rather than to note the change [behaviour] — the
report went out with the same issue two days later [impact]. When feedback is given on something
concrete like formatting, the useful response is usually just 'got it, I'll fix it' — save the
pushback for when you think the feedback itself is wrong, and say so directly rather than
explaining around it [next step]."

## A full-period competency assessment (Standard/Deep)

> **Delivery.** Consistently met sprint commitments this quarter — 11 of 12 stories delivered on the
> date estimated at planning. The one miss (the reporting API in week 6) was flagged three days in
> advance rather than discovered at the deadline, which let the team replan around it. Keep doing
> this: the early flag is more valuable than a perfect hit rate.
>
> **Collaboration.** Reviews other people's PRs promptly (median under 4 hours this quarter) but
> comments tend to focus on style over substance — two logic bugs went through review in March and
> April that a closer read would likely have caught. Suggest spending the first pass on logic, the
> second on style.

Notice the length discipline: 2-5 sentences, one concrete number or instance per point, ending on
what to do next rather than just a verdict.
