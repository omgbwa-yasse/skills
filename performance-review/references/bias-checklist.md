# Bias checklist

Run this before finalising any review, especially a Deep annual/career one.

## Recency bias

**Symptom:** every example cited comes from the last few weeks, none from months 1-9 of the review
period.
**Check:** if there's a running SBI log, sample from across the whole period deliberately — pull at
least one example from the first third, the middle, and the last third before writing the
assessment.

## Halo / horn effect

**Symptom:** one strong (or weak) trait colours every other competency's rating in the same
direction, without independent evidence for each.
**Check:** for each competency rated, ask "what's the specific example for *this* competency" — if
the answer is a situation that actually demonstrates a different competency, the rating is borrowing
credibility from elsewhere.

## Similarity bias

**Symptom:** people who work or communicate similarly to the reviewer get more generous readings of
ambiguous situations than people who don't.
**Check:** for a borderline call, ask whether the same behaviour from someone with a different
working style would be read the same way — a quiet, methodical worker's silence in a meeting and a
communicative worker's silence in the same meeting shouldn't be interpreted differently without a
reason tied to the actual impact.

## Consistency across a team

**Symptom:** two people with comparable performance receive noticeably different overall
characterisations, with no example-level difference that explains the gap.
**Check:** SKILL.md's non-negotiable on this — literally compare the write-ups side by side for
comparable roles before finalising, not just within each review in isolation.

## Attribution error

**Symptom:** a systemic or team-level problem (unclear requirements, an overloaded queue, a broken
tool) gets attributed to an individual's behaviour instead.
**Check:** before writing corrective feedback, ask whether the same situation would have produced
the same outcome for a different, equally competent person on the team. If yes, the feedback belongs
to the team or the system, not the individual.

## Grade inflation from conflict-avoidance

**Symptom:** every write-up leans positive, improvement areas are phrased so softly they don't
register as needing action, and the overall pattern doesn't match known performance issues on the
team.
**Check:** for each "improvement area," ask whether a reader who only saw that sentence would
understand it as something to change — if it reads as a compliment with a minor caveat, rewrite it
plainly.
